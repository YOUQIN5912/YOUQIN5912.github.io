I study how atomic arrangements and defects determine the behaviour of functional materials. My research connects the synthesis and processing of bulk and nanoscale solids with structural measurements and calculations. At the University of Oslo, I focus on how perovskite oxides respond to changes in temperature and gas atmosphere during electrochemical energy conversion.

## Complex functional and energy materials {#functional-materials}

### Nanoparticle exsolution in perovskites {#nanoparticle-exsolution}

Metal nanoparticles can precipitate from perovskites under reducing conditions and form interfaces relevant to electrocatalysis. I study how the host composition and point defects affect when particles form, where they grow and how the host oxide changes during reduction. In situ synchrotron diffraction and X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) help follow the host lattice and the transition-metal environment.

- [Exsolution of metal nanoparticles by control of point defects]({{ '/publications/exsolution-point-defects/' | relative_url }})
- [Multiple stages of metal exsolution from nonstoichiometric perovskites]({{ '/publications/multiple-stages-of-metal-exsolution/' | relative_url }})

### Hydration and defects in proton ceramics {#proton-ceramics}

In ceramic electrolytes and electrodes, I investigate how dopants, oxygen vacancies and extended defects affect hydration. In acceptor-doped barium zirconate, local structural measurements and atomistic models help explain how nickel changes the defect environment and reduces proton uptake.

- [Nickel-induced lattice defects limit proton uptake in barium zirconate electrolytes]({{ '/publications/nickel-induced-lattice-defects/' | relative_url }})

## Computational materials science {#computational-materials}

I use density functional theory (DFT) and *ab initio* molecular dynamics to study defect energetics, local coordination and structural fluctuations in functional solids. For chemically disordered crystals, I use special quasirandom structures to represent site occupancies and compare predicted pair correlations with experimental data. This comparison helps identify which local distortions and cation arrangements are consistent with the measurements.

- [Special quasirandom structures and local disorder in Bi₀.₅K₀.₅TiO₃]({{ '/publications/quasirandom-structures-bkt/' | relative_url }})

## Local structure with X-rays and neutrons {#local-structure}

Local atomic arrangements can differ substantially from the average crystal structure. I use powder diffraction and Rietveld refinement for the average structure, and synchrotron X-ray and neutron total scattering with pair distribution function (PDF) analysis for local distortions and short-range order. XANES and EXAFS provide complementary information about the oxidation state and coordination around selected elements.

My work with Katharine Page at Oak Ridge National Laboratory and the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, focused on using X-rays and neutrons to study structure–property relationships in disordered and nanostructured materials. These approaches also inform my current work on perovskite oxides under changing gas atmospheres.
